Methodology

A hierarchical 2D–3D matching pipeline

One algorithm, three stages, applied without modification across social, biological, and molecular graphs.

01

2D Topological Filter — GNCCP

Formulates matching as a Weighted Common Subgraph (WCS) problem over the partial permutation polytope, solved via the Graduated Non-Convexity and Concavity Procedure (GNCCP) with Frank-Wolfe optimization.

  • Rank-one factorization of the co-matching indicator matrix U reduces gradient computation from O(n³) to O(n²) per evaluation.
  • Frank-Wolfe direction is solved exactly via the Hungarian algorithm at each inner iteration.
  • Frank-Wolfe duality gap provides a certified early-termination criterion.
  • Output: a bijective node correspondence matrix X* and a topological similarity score.
U = X · 1ₙₓₙ · Xᵀ = r rᵀ  →  O(MN² + M²N) reduced to O(MN + M²)
02

3D Spatial Verification — Kabsch Alignment

The bijective correspondence from Stage 1 is reused directly: matched atom or node pairs are extracted, centered, and aligned with a single closed-form rigid rotation.

  • Cross-covariance matrix is fixed at 3×3 regardless of the number of matched points, so its SVD costs O(1).
  • Total Stage 2 cost is O(n) — dominated by forming the covariance matrix, not by the alignment itself.
  • Output: optimal rotation, translation, and RMSD — the physical plausibility check that Stage 1 alone cannot provide.
03

Hybrid Kernel Fusion

Topological similarity (k₂D) and geometric similarity (k₃D) are combined into a single weighted kernel, cross-validated per target domain.

  • k_hybrid = (1 − β)·k₂D + β·k₃D, with β tuned per application.
  • Stage 1 acts as a cheap search-space reduction gate; only candidates above a similarity threshold proceed to the O(n) Stage 2 check.
  • Downstream classification (e.g. SVM) uses the fused Gram matrix for tasks such as motif verification or structure retrieval.

Why one pipeline generalizes across domains

The formulation only requires a weighted adjacency matrix and, optionally, 3D coordinates — nothing domain-specific. A temporal social-interaction graph, a neuron connectivity graph, and a crystal bond graph are all just weighted adjacency matrices to Stage 1. Stage 2 activates only where 3D coordinates exist (Drosophila connectomes, MOF crystal structures), and is skipped for domains like social networks where no physical embedding applies.
